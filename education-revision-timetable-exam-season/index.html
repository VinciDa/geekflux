How to build a revision timetable that survives exam season.

Exam calendars differ by country. Panic looks the same everywhere. Here is a timetable that bends without breaking.

By Maya Chen Published September 5, 2026 Read 10 min
M
Maya Chen
GeekFlux Research Desk — learning science, attention, and habit design for students and working adults.
Quick answer

Work backward from exam dates, map topics to days, schedule spaced reviews, protect buffer weeks, and cap daily new learning. A surviving timetable is 70% review and mixed practice in the final stretch — not endless new chapters.

Step 1: List every exam date and syllabus chunk

Write the real dates: SAT Saturday, ACT window, GCSE/A-Level papers, HSC/VCE blocks, Leaving Cert sittings, midterms, finals. Under each, list examinable topics — from the official outline, not your vibes.

Step 2: Count available study days

Subtract travel, work shifts, sports, and religious holidays. If you have 40 real days and 80 topics, you cannot “do everything deeply.” You must triage.

Step 3: Assign first-pass learning, then spacing

  1. First pass: learn or re-learn the topic (notes, class, textbook).
  2. Schedule reviews at +1, +3, +7, +14 days.
  3. Color-code subjects so no day is five hours of one colour.
  4. Cap new learning at 60–90 minutes on weekdays; protect evenings for review.

Step 4: Build the final two weeks differently

Stop opening new chapters. Switch to mixed past papers, timed sections, and weak-topic repair. Interleaving feels worse and predicts better exam transfer.

Step 5: Add buffers like an adult

  • One empty half-day per week for overflow.
  • A full buffer weekend before the first high-stakes paper.
  • Sleep as a scheduled block — memory consolidation is not optional.

Regional notes (same method, different labels)

  • US/Canada: SAT/ACT benefit from spaced vocab + timed sections; AP/IB and provincial exams need topic maps.
  • UK/Ireland: GCSE/A-Level/Leaving Cert — past papers are the curriculum in disguise; schedule them early.
  • Australia: HSC/VCE/ATAR — weight by assessment contribution; do not ignore school-assessed tasks while chasing the final exam.

A timetable survives when it expects disruption. Design for the week you will actually live, not the week you wish you were.

Frequently asked questions

How far ahead should I start a revision timetable?

For major exams, 8–12 weeks is ideal. Four weeks still works if you cut new material early and prioritize high-weight topics.

Should every subject get equal time?

No. Weight time by exam value and current weakness. A strong subject needs maintenance reviews; a weak one needs more retrieval practice.

What if I miss two days?

Slide unfinished reviews forward; do not delete the plan. Compress new learning, not review.

Paper calendar or app?

Either works. Apps win when reminders and interval math would otherwise fail.
